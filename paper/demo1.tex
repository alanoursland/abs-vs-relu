This demonstration explores the theoretical relationship between Gaussian clusters and linear separators with absolute value activation functions. We compare single-layer neural networks using Absolute Value (Abs) and Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) activation functions in their ability to approximate the Mahalanobis distance of points in a 2D Gaussian cluster.

We generated 2D Gaussian data with specified mean and covariance matrix, calculating the true Mahalanobis distance for each point. Two single-node neural networks were implemented. One uses an Abs activation and the other uses ReLU. Both models were trained to predict the Mahalanobis distance of input points. Training utilized Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) optimization over a fixed number of epochs.

The ReLU model approximates the Mahalanobis distance by positioning the hyperplane at a distance from the cluster that just happens to approximate the correct value. The Abs model usually bisects the dimension of highest variance. The weight W approximates the scaled eigenvector of the largest primary component of the Gaussian. However, the Abs model sometimes falls into the same local minimum as the ReLU function. These cases are demonstrated in Figure 1 and Figure 2.
